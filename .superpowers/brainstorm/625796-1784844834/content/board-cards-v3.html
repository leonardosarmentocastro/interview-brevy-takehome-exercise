My challenge to you — every element must change a decision, or it's noise

Card elementThe decision it lets an operator make at a glanceMy verdict
Type + amountTriage by size; know the family of problem● must-have
Urgency border + SLA pill"Pick this next / this can wait" without opening it● must-have
Why it's here (leak / escalate / recommend)Is this mine to decide, or a handoff? The product's whole point● must-have
Recommended action + one-line "because"Confirm vs investigate — turns the operator from investigator into confirmer. Highest-leverage element on the card● must-have
Risk score + high-value flagPolicy hinges on it (risk gates auto-resolve; >$2000 gets extra care) — it can flip the recommendation● earns place
Related open tickets (same customer)Batch them; avoid two operators making conflicting calls on one customer● earns place
Confidence on the recommendationHow hard to lean on the suggestion — but risks false precision● debatable
Lifetime detail, history, merchant, ids, timestampsBackground — needed to investigate, not to triage● cut to detail view

Three things worth arguing about: (1) the biggest help isn't more context — it's the recommended action + because, which is only possible because we did the policy reasoning. (2) a leak card legitimately shows less — it can't recommend, and that absence ("policy contradicts itself") is itself the most useful thing on it. Don't fake a recommendation to fill space. (3) the line between card and detail is: card = decide-or-defer; detail = investigate.
